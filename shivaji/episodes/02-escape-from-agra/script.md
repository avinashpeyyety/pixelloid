# Ep 02 — Escape from Agra (narration)

**Sources/history notes:** After the Treaty of Purandar (1665) Shivaji agreed to attend Aurangzeb's court and reached Agra with his son Sambhaji (born 1657, so about nine). At the durbar of 12 May 1666 (the emperor's birthday celebration) he was placed among lower-ranked nobles, protested openly and left; he was then kept under guard in his lodging (under Ram Singh's responsibility, with imperial guards posted). He feigned illness and sent out baskets of sweets/fruit as charity; on c. 17 Aug 1666 he escaped. That he and Sambhaji left **hidden in the baskets** is the popular account (contemporary sources differ in detail), so it is told as "the story goes". Hiroji Farzand lay in his bed and Madari Mehtar (a young servant) sat at his feet, then both slipped away. Shivaji travelled back in disguise as a wandering ascetic; Sambhaji was left in Mathura with trusted Brahmin families and brought home later. Shivaji reached Rajgad in the autumn of 1666 (dates in sources vary; we say "weeks later"). Jijabai not recognising him in disguise is tradition. Aurangzeb is shown as a dignified distant emperor, never a caricature; no violence in this episode.

1. Long ago, in the year 1666, Shivaji Maharaj made a long journey north, all the way to the great city of Agra, where the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb lived.
2. A hard treaty had made peace for a while. As part of that peace, Shivaji agreed to visit the emperor's court, and he took his son Sambhaji, who was just nine years old.
3. Before they left, his mother, Jijabai, held his hands and said softly, "Go carefully, and come home to us."
4. In Agra, the court was full of carved pillars, soft carpets, and nobles standing in long rows. It was the emperor's birthday.
5. But Shivaji Maharaj was asked to stand far back, among less important nobles. This was not the respect he had been promised. He spoke up bravely, and walked away from the court.
6. After that, the emperor would not let him leave. Soldiers guarded the house where Shivaji and Sambhaji stayed, day and night. They were far from home, in a kind of golden cage.
7. Shivaji thought, and thought. Then he had a clever idea. He pretended to be very ill, and stayed in bed for many days.
8. To pray for his health, he sent out big baskets of sweets and fruit, as gifts for holy men and the poor. Every evening the baskets went out of the gate. At first the guards peeked inside. After many days, they stopped looking.
9. Now, here is the part people love most. One August evening, the story goes, Shivaji and Sambhaji curled up inside two of those baskets, and were carried right past the guards.
10. Back in the house, a loyal friend named Hiroji Farzand lay in Shivaji's bed under a blanket, with only one hand showing, wearing Shivaji's ring.
11. A young helper, Madari Mehtar, sat beside him, gently pressing his feet. Later, the two slipped quietly away, whispering to the guards, "Shh. Maharaj is resting."
12. By the time anyone found the empty bed, Shivaji Maharaj was far, far away.
13. He travelled in disguise, dressed as a wandering holy man, along dusty roads and quiet rivers. To keep Sambhaji safe, he left him with trusted friends in the town of Mathura.
14. After many weeks on the road, Shivaji Maharaj saw the green Sahyadri hills once more.
15. The story goes that when a holy man came to her door, Jijabai did not know him at first. Then he bowed to touch her feet, and she saw her son's face. Her eyes filled with happy tears.
16. Some months later, young Sambhaji came home safely too, and the saffron flag flew over Rajgad fort.
17. This adventure was not won with swords. It was won with patience, a clever plan, and friends who were brave and loyal.
