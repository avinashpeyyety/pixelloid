# ORBITAL HEXES
## Episode 1: "The Stick-Shakers"

Status: **v2 polish draft (2026-11-10) — awaiting Avinash's script review.** No keyframes, no Orion render, no animation until approved.
Tone: contemporary deadpan satire (think dry festival short, not kids-history). Target 3:00–3:30.
Voice: **Orion only.** One Orion narrator frames the piece; character lines are performed by Orion in-character (no other voices). See open question Q1 in `../../NEXT.md`.
Seed: v1 script seed (commit 6bfeaa6, 2026-11-05). Changes in v2: cold-open narrator line, scene numbers, a tighter mid-beat for Petro, the mesh-terminal payoff, and a closing narrator tag. Original dialogue kept where it already landed.

**1. COLD OPEN — BLACK**

A single soft *beep*, far away. Then a slow pan across a star field until one tiny dot crawls across it.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Four hundred and fifty kilometres up, a satellite completes its scheduled pass. It carries weather data, a few million text messages, and one farmer's video of a goat. It has never cursed anyone.

*(beat)*

Down below, opinions differ.

**2. EXT. FORMER PROVINCE — CLEARING — DUSK**

A dusty clearing. Half a dozen men in mismatched tunics stand in a circle, waving sticks and fists at the sky. In the middle, **CHIEF RITUALIST BROK** wears a bathrobe and a paper crown labelled "People's Spiritual Liaison."

**BROK**
*(shouting upward)*
We see you, sky-spirits! Your invisible birds circle and curse the people! Lower your hexes!

**ELDER MIRA**
*(squinting)*
I don't see any birds.

**BROK**
That's how you know they're working. Real hexes are invisible. The old towers used to make noise. These ones just… compute.

A younger man, **PETRO**, lowers his stick.

**PETRO**
Comrade Brok, the engineer who left last year said those are just data relays. No curses. Just packets.

**BROK**
*(outraged)*
Packets! The same packets that made the grain silos stop listening to us? The same packets that moved the clinics to places with electricity? Classic spirit-trick.

He points dramatically at nothing.

**BROK**
Shake harder! If we stop shaking, they win!

The circle resumes half-hearted stick-waving. Elder Mira shakes hers at a slightly different patch of sky, just in case.

**3. EXT. TREE LINE — CONTINUOUS**

Two teenagers watch from a fallen log, eating something from a foil packet. One has a cracked phone face-down on her knee.

**TEEN 1**
They've been at this since the radio died.

**TEEN 2**
My uncle still thinks the satellites stole his signal on purpose. He won't touch the mesh terminal because "it listens with both ears."

**TEEN 1**
Did you tell him the terminal runs on the same constellation he's yelling at?

**TEEN 2**
Yeah. He said that's exactly what a cursed satellite would want me to think.

They watch Brok trip over his own stick and recover with enormous dignity.

**4. EXT. CLEARING — CONTINUOUS**

**BROK**
*(calling out)*
We reject your orbital sorcery! Return the meaningful technology! We had meaningful technology! It had gears!

**PETRO**
*(quietly)*
It also had a waiting list and no spare parts.

**BROK**
Silence! The spirits are listening!

**PETRO**
*(even more quietly)*
That part's actually true. They're just not interested.

**5. EXT. CLEARING — LATER — NIGHT FALLING**

The ritual winds down. Men drift off, rubbing their shoulders. Brok stays, crown askew, glaring upward.

Elder Mira shuffles past him toward the village, then stops.

**ELDER MIRA**
Brok. My granddaughter's clinic call is at eight.

**BROK**
*(not looking down)*
Through the cursed box?

**ELDER MIRA**
Through the cursed box. Shake quietly until then.

She leaves. Brok considers this, lowers his stick a few centimetres, then raises it again — slightly less high.

**6. EXT. TREE LINE — NIGHT**

The teens' phone lights up: *1 bar.* A tiny cheer between them. Above, a pinprick of light crosses the sky.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The satellite completes another pass. It delivers the clinic call, the weather, and — once again — the goat. It still does not know it has been accused of witchcraft.

*(beat)*

It will be back in ninety-four minutes.

**FADE OUT.**
